Golden Hour Caprese Pasta with Blistered Peaches & Burrata

Happy Friday, everyone! There is something magical about the light on an August evening. As the sun starts its slow dip toward the horizon, the kitchen glows in that perfect "golden hour" hue. Today, I’m leaning into that warmth with a dish that celebrates the peak of summer produce. We’re taking the classic Caprese and giving it a sunset makeover with juicy yellow peaches and creamy burrata. It’s light, vibrant, and takes less than 20 minutes—perfect for a late summer dinner on the patio.

Prep: 10 mins Cook: 15 mins Servings: 4

Ingredients

  • 1 lb (450g) Bucatini or Spaghetti
  • 3 large Ripe peaches, sliced into thick wedges
  • 2 cups Heirloom cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 3 cloves Garlic, thinly sliced
  • 2 balls (8oz) Fresh Burrata cheese
  • 1/4 cup Extra virgin olive oil
  • 1/2 tsp Red pepper flakes (optional)
  • 1 bunch Fresh basil leaves, torn
  • 2 tbsp Balsamic glaze (for drizzling)
  • Sea salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. Boil the Pasta: Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a boil. Cook the bucatini according to package directions until just al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup of pasta water before draining.
  2. Blister the Peaches & Tomatoes: While the pasta cooks, heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the peach wedges in a single layer. Sear for 2 minutes on one side until they begin to caramelize and brown. Flip them carefully.
  3. Aromatics: Add the halved cherry tomatoes and sliced garlic to the skillet with the peaches. Cook for another 3-4 minutes until the tomatoes begin to burst and release their juices, creating a light sauce. Stir in the red pepper flakes.
  4. Emulsify: Reduce the heat to low. Toss the drained pasta into the skillet. Pour in half of the reserved pasta water and toss vigorously with tongs. The starch from the water will combine with the peach juices and olive oil to coat the noodles. Add more water if it seems dry.
  5. Assemble: Season generously with salt and pepper. Gently fold in half of the torn basil.
  6. The Finishing Touch: Plate the pasta into shallow bowls. Carefully tear the burrata balls into pieces and nestle them on top of the warm pasta. The heat from the noodles will make the cream spill out.
  7. Serve: Drizzle with balsamic glaze, top with the remaining fresh basil, and a final glug of olive oil. Serve immediately.

Chef's Tips for Success

  • Peach Ripeness: Look for peaches that are fragrant but still slightly firm. If they are too mushy, they will fall apart in the pan rather than caramelizing.
  • The Pasta Water Secret: Never skip reserving the pasta water! It's the liquid gold that turns a dry skillet of fruit and veg into a silky, cohesive sauce.
  • Burrata Temperature: Take your burrata out of the fridge 20 minutes before serving. It’s much more flavorful and creamy when served at room temperature rather than ice-cold.
