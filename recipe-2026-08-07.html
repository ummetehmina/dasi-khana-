Summer Gold: Grilled Peach & Burrata Panzanella

Friday, August 7, 2026

Happy Friday, everyone! We have officially hit that glorious "sweet spot" of August. The humidity is thick, the cicadas are singing their hearts out, and the peaches are so heavy on the branches they’re practically begging to be picked. To celebrate this first Friday of the month, I’m leaning into the heat with a dish that requires minimal stove time but delivers maximum flavor.

Today’s recipe is a twist on the classic Italian bread salad. We’re swapping out some of the traditional tomatoes for charred, caramelized peaches and pairing them with the king of creaminess: Burrata. It’s sweet, salty, smoky, and bright. It’s summer on a plate.

Prep Time: 15 mins Cook Time: 10 mins Servings: 4

Ingredients

  • The Bread: 4 cups of rustic sourdough bread, cut into 1-inch cubes.
  • The Fruit: 3 large, firm-but-ripe peaches, pitted and sliced into thick wedges.
  • The Cheese: 2 balls of fresh Burrata (approx. 8oz total).
  • The Greens: 2 cups baby arugula or wild greens.
  • The Crunch: 1/2 small red onion, very thinly sliced.
  • The Herbs: 1/2 cup fresh basil leaves, torn.
  • The Vinaigrette:
    • 1/4 cup extra virgin olive oil
    • 2 tablespoons white balsamic vinegar
    • 1 tablespoon honey (use Hot Honey for a kick!)
    • 1 clove garlic, minced
    • Salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste

Instructions

  1. Toast the Bread: Preheat your oven to 375°F (190°C). Toss the sourdough cubes with a drizzle of olive oil and a pinch of salt. Spread on a baking sheet and toast for 8-10 minutes until golden and crisp on the edges but still slightly chewy in the middle. Set aside to cool.
  2. Grill the Peaches: Heat a grill pan or outdoor grill to medium-high heat. Lightly brush the peach wedges with olive oil. Grill for 2-3 minutes per side until distinct char marks appear and the fruit softens slightly. Remove and let cool.
  3. Prepare the Dressing: In a small jar or bowl, whisk together the olive oil, white balsamic vinegar, honey, minced garlic, salt, and pepper until emulsified.
  4. The "Mingle": In a large mixing bowl, combine the toasted bread cubes, grilled peaches, sliced red onion, and half of the torn basil. Drizzle 3/4 of the dressing over the mixture and toss gently. Let it sit for about 5 minutes; this allows the bread to soak up those delicious juices.
  5. Assemble: Gently fold in the baby arugula. Transfer the salad to a large serving platter.
  6. The Finishing Touch: Break the Burrata balls open over the top of the salad, letting the creamy centers spill out. Drizzle with the remaining dressing, garnish with the rest of the basil, and a final crack of black pepper.

Pro-Tips for Success

Bread Choice: Use day-old bread if possible. The drier the bread, the better it absorbs the vinaigrette without becoming mushy.

Peach Ripeness: If your peaches are too soft, they will fall apart on the grill. Look for fruit that gives just slightly when pressed with your thumb.

Make it a Meal: This salad is hearty on its own, but it pairs beautifully with grilled sea bass or a chilled glass of Rosé.
